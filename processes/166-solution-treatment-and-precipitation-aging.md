## Description

An alloy is heated to dissolve strengthening phases, quenched to retain a supersaturated solid solution, and aged so fine precipitates form.

## Strengths and weaknesses

Can greatly increase yield strength, creep resistance, or elevated-temperature capability while preserving the alloy's underlying density and corrosion characteristics. Only works for suitable alloy systems, and poor temperature, quench, or aging control can cause distortion, residual stress, underaging, or overaging.

## Examples

Aluminum aircraft structures, nickel-superalloy turbine components, titanium aerospace parts, copper-alloy springs.
