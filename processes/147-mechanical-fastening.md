## Description

Parts are joined with discrete hardware or interlocking geometry rather than fusion or adhesive.

## Strengths and weaknesses

Broadly applicable, often reversible and inspectable. Adds weight or hardware, requires holes or precise tolerances, and joints can loosen, creep, or fatigue.

## Variants

### Bolts and screws

Reversible and inspectable, but they add weight, require holes, and can loosen.

### Riveting

Reliable for thin sheet and difficult-to-weld materials, but requires hole preparation and often two-sided access.

### Clinching

Locally deforms sheet layers into an interlock without separate fasteners. Fast but lower-strength and generally requires two-sided tools.

### Snap fits

Flexible features engage mating geometry without separate hardware. Reduce assembly cost but are sensitive to creep, fatigue, and tolerance.

### Press and interference fits

Dimensionally overlapping components are forced together. Compact but require tight tolerances and may be difficult to disassemble.

### Shrink fits

Heating or cooling temporarily changes dimensions before assembly. Strong concentric joints but added thermal process complexity.

## Examples

Aircraft structures, machinery, consumer products, electronics.
