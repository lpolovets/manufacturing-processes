## Description

An electric arc melts base and filler metal to fuse a joint. Major variants differ in electrode, shielding, and deposition rate.

## Strengths and weaknesses

Broadly applicable, from portable field repair to automated production. Speed, quality, fume generation, and operator skill vary strongly by variant.

## Variants

### Shielded metal arc (stick)

A flux-coated consumable electrode creates the arc and deposits weld metal. Inexpensive and portable but slow and operator-dependent.

### Gas metal arc (MIG)

A continuously fed wire melts under shielding gas. Fast and automatable but sensitive to wind, fit-up, and cleanliness.

### Gas tungsten arc (TIG)

A nonconsumable tungsten electrode creates a clean, precise arc. High-quality welds but relatively slow and skill-intensive.

### Flux-cored arc

A tubular flux-filled wire provides high deposition rates. Works well on heavy steel but creates more fumes and slag.

### Submerged arc

A wire welds beneath granular flux. Very high deposition rates but mainly suits long flat or horizontal seams.

## Examples

Structures, vehicles, pipelines, pressure vessels, heavy equipment.
