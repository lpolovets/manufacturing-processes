## Description
The purest multi-day storage bet: batteries that discharge for ~100 hours by rusting iron, then charge by un-rusting it. Iron is the cheapest energy-storage medium available in battery form, so energy capacity capex targets ~$20/kWh — a number no lithium roadmap approaches — at the price of ~40–50% round-trip efficiency and very low power density. This is storage built to sit mostly full and carry grids through wind droughts. (Chemistry detail lives in the Battery Chemistries sheet.)

## Strengths and weaknesses
Radically cheap energy capacity from a boring supply chain; non-flammable; naturally suited to the 10–100+ hour band lithium cannot economically reach. Weaknesses: half the energy is lost to hydrogen evolution and air-electrode overpotentials; acres per megawatt; slow response consigns it to energy-only services; air-electrode durability across thousands of cycles is the persistent engineering risk; and the multi-day market it serves barely exists in today's tariffs.

## Examples
Form Energy: Cambridge (Minnesota) first commercial install, Great River Energy and Xcel projects, Weirton (West Virginia) factory; academic alkaline-iron-electrode lineage back to NiFe.

## Economic profile
LCOS at 100-hour duty beats every alternative except hydrogen caverns — without needing geology. The commercial risk is timing: revenue mechanisms for multi-day firming (capacity accreditation for long-duration, reliability contracts) are only now being invented. Form's strategy of selling insurance-like reliability to utilities, rather than merchant arbitrage, is the honest match to the physics.
