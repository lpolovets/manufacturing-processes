## Description
The discharge half of the hydrogen chain: convert stored hydrogen back to electricity through fuel cells (PEM or solid-oxide, 45–60% efficient) or hydrogen-capable gas turbines (35–60%, mature machines burning blends today and pure H2 on vendor roadmaps). This is what turns a cavern into a power plant — and it is the component that decides whether "hydrogen storage" means anything for grids rather than just for industry.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Turbines bring gigawatt scale, black-start capability, and a century of fleet experience; fuel cells bring quiet, modular, high-efficiency conversion at distribution scale. Weaknesses: conversion efficiency stacks multiplicatively with electrolysis to yield the infamous 30–40% round trip; hydrogen combustion brings NOx and flashback engineering; fuel-cell capex and stack life still trail turbines at scale; and utilization is inherently low for seasonal duty, so capex per kW must carry near-idle economics.

## When to use
Deploy hydrogen-to-power as rarely-run firm capacity paid to exist — seasonal backup, wind-drought insurance, capacity-market and reliability contracts — with H2-ready retrofits of existing gas turbines as the capital-efficient entry, and fuel cells where distribution-scale, quiet, modular units fit. Underwrite it per kW-year against the real competition: gas peakers with CCS or offsets, not batteries. Never build it as an arbitrage asset — the 30–40% round trip and single-digit capacity factors kill energy-market economics — and for anything cycled daily or weekly, lithium or flow batteries are the answer.

## Examples
Intermountain Power (Utah) — 840 MW turbines running 30% hydrogen with 100% capability, fed by ACES caverns; Mitsubishi, GE Vernova, Siemens Energy H2 turbine programs; Bloom and FuelCell Energy stationary fleets; Germany's planned "H2-ready" peaker auctions.

## Economic profile
These are capacity assets, not energy assets: they earn by existing (capacity markets, reliability contracts) and run rarely. The competition is not batteries but gas peakers with offsets or CCS — the question is which decarbonized firm-power option clears cheapest per kW-year. Turbine retrofits of existing gas fleets are the capital-efficient path that keeps this category alive.
