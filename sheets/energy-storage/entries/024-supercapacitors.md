## Description
Energy stored electrostatically in the double layer at a carbon electrode's surface — no chemical reaction at all. Supercapacitors charge and discharge in seconds, endure a million cycles, and work from -40 to +65 °C, but store an order of magnitude less energy per kilogram than batteries. Hybrid lithium-ion capacitors split the difference. They are power components: the electrical equivalent of a spring, not a fuel tank.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Near-perfect efficiency, essentially unlimited cycle life, instant response, precise state-of-charge from voltage, and graceful aging. Weaknesses: $/kWh runs into the thousands; self-discharge of percent-per-day scales poorly beyond minutes of duty; voltage sags linearly with discharge, forcing wide-range power electronics; and every application niche is under pressure from ever-cheaper high-power lithium cells.

## When to use
Specify supercapacitors for seconds-scale power duty at extreme cycle counts or temperatures — wind-pitch backup, tram and crane regeneration, truck engine-start, ride-through bridging, grid-forming inertia — where a million cycles from -40 to +65 °C would destroy any battery, and hybridize them in front of a battery when a load mixes sharp pulses with sustained draw. Size energy in seconds to a minute or two, never more. Beyond that, avoid them: percent-per-day self-discharge and thousands of $/kWh make minutes-plus duty untenable, and high-power lithium cells keep annexing the marginal niches — the recurring failure mode is pitching a capacitor where a battery belongs.

## Examples
Wind-turbine pitch backup (the classic design win), regenerative braking in trams and port cranes, engine-start modules for trucks, grid inertia pilots; vendors Skeleton (curved graphene), Maxwell (absorbed into Tesla and back out), Eaton, LS Materials.

## Economic profile
A stable, profitable components business (~$1B-class market) selling reliability and cycle life into industrial niches — not a storage market in the grid sense. Growth theses hinge on grid-forming inertia services and hybridization with batteries; the recurring failure mode is pitching a capacitor where a battery belongs.
