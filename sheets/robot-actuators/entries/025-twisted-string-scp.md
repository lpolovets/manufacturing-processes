## Description
Two muscles made from string. Twisted-string actuators (TSA) spin a pair of cords with a tiny high-speed motor; twisting shortens the bundle, converting cheap RPM into high linear force — a transmission that weighs grams and costs cents, with ratios that grow as it twists. Supercoiled polymer (SCP) muscles go further: nylon fishing line, twisted until it coils, contracts several percent when heated — a 2014 discovery that turned sewing thread into a muscle with 100× the work density of biological tissue.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Absurd force-per-gram and cost profiles; TSA turns any micro-motor into a tendon puller, ideal for prosthetic hands and wearables. Weaknesses: TSA strings fatigue and the nonlinear ratio complicates control; travel is limited and speed modest. SCP muscles inherit SMA's thermal curse — single-digit efficiency, cooling-limited hertz bandwidth — plus polymer creep; they are science-fair spectacular and application-rare.

## When to use
Use a twisted-string actuator when a gram-scale, cent-scale mechanism must pull like a tendon: prosthetic and robotic fingers, exo-gloves, cable tensioners — anywhere a tiny high-RPM motor plus two cords replaces a gearbox, and the naturally rising ratio suits grasps that start fast and end forceful. Budget for string replacement as a wear item and accept the nonlinear ratio in your controller. Avoid TSA for long-travel or high-cycle continuous axes, and avoid SCP muscles for products entirely — single-digit thermal efficiency and hertz-class bandwidth confine them to research until a non-Joule heating path appears; for controlled linear force at scale, a screw or voice coil remains the tool.

## Examples
TSA: prosthetic hands and exo-gloves in research (and consumer products quietly — some e-bike and cable-tensioning mechanisms), NASA tendon experiments; SCP: University of Texas/Baughman lab's nylon muscles, haptic and micro-robotics demos, textile-integrated actuator research.

## Economic profile
TSA is an underrated engineering trick with real product wins available today in hands, grippers, and wearables — anywhere a gram-scale motor must pull like a tendon. SCP remains materials-science upside without a thermal exit: watch for photonic or chemical (non-Joule) heating breakthroughs before believing product claims. Combined, they illustrate the field's pattern: the transmission innovations ship; the thermal muscles wait.
