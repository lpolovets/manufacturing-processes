## Description
Crystals that flex under voltage: piezo ceramics deform by ~0.1% of their length, but do so with sub-nanometer resolution, microsecond response, and enormous force. Stacks deliver tens of microns at kilonewtons; amplified flexures trade force for millimeter strokes; and ultrasonic/stick-slip motors chain microscopic steps into unlimited travel at surprising speed. Wherever positioning is measured in nanometers — microscopy, photonics, semiconductor metrology — piezo is the only game.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Unmatched resolution and bandwidth, vacuum- and cryo-compatible, no magnetic fields, holds position unpowered (stacks), immense stiffness. Weaknesses: native stroke is microscopic — everything else is amplification with its costs; hysteresis and creep demand closed-loop sensing for accuracy; high drive voltages (100–1000 V); brittle ceramics hate tension and shock; and ultrasonic motors wear at friction interfaces.

## Examples
Every AFM and confocal microscope stage (Physik Instrumente, npoint), camera autofocus ultrasonic ring motors (Canon USM lineage), fuel-injector piezo stacks (billions of firings), fiber-alignment stages, inkjet printheads — arguably the highest-volume piezo actuator of all.

## Economic profile
A profitable precision oligopoly (PI, Cedrat, Noliac/CTS) at instrument-industry margins, plus automotive-scale stack production for injectors proving the ceramics can be cheap at volume. Robotics touches piezo at the extremes: micro-manipulation, surgical micro-tools, and any claim of nanometer dexterity. It will never move a limb — and never needs to.
