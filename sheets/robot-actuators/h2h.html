Head-to-head: the usual short list

Most joint arguments come down to the same handful of architectures — the ones below are what actually compete for a given axis. Exotic and research approaches (piezo, voice coils, artificial muscles) solve different problems and live in the explorer.

ArchitectureTorque densityPrecisionBackdrive & contactCostPick it when
Servo + harmonicHighFine, zero backlashPoor; hard impacts can ratchet$1k–10kWrists and distal joints needing 50–160:1 in minimum mass and axial depth, with controlled trajectories and rare impacts.
Servo + planetaryHighModerate; arcminute backlashGood at low ratio$100–1kThe default axis — cheapest torque density at any ratio one or two stages can reach; wheels, drivetrains, and general positioning that tolerates backlash.
Servo + cycloidalHighFine, torsionally stiffPoor$1k–10kShocked high-torque proximal joints — robot bases and shoulders, humanoid hips and knees — where 500% momentary overload is the spec that matters.
Quasi-direct driveHigh; static holds run hotModerateExcellent; torque sensed from current$100–1kContact-rich dynamic joints — legs and dynamic arms that strike the world and must feel it. The modern legged default.
Ball/roller screwExtreme (linear)Fine to sub-micronNone; lead screws self-lock$100–1kThe joint is really a linear force problem — knees, ankles, presses, clamps: kilonewtons from a modest motor, but contact is sensed, never felt.
Servo-hydraulicExtreme at the cylinderModeratePoor; valve-mediated$10k+Tens of kilonewtons per axis in dirty, hot, shock-laden environments where one power plant amortizes across many joints — excavator class and up.
PneumaticModerateCoarse; endpoints, not mid-strokeExcellent, intrinsically compliant<$100Binary motion against stops at high cycle rates — clamp, eject, gripper — where plant air exists and the axis must cost tens of dollars.
