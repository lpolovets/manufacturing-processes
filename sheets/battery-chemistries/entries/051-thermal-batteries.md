## Description
A battery that is inert until ignited: electrodes separated by a salt electrolyte that is solid — and non-conductive — at room temperature. A pyrotechnic charge melts the salt in under a second, activating a battery that then delivers ferocious power for minutes. Modern types use lithium-silicon/iron disulfide couples. Sealed and solid, they store for 20+ years with zero maintenance and activate reliably after decades of neglect.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Decades of dormant shelf life with instant, all-conditions activation (-55 to +75 °C storage); very high power density; immune to vibration, spin, and acceleration (fired from artillery shells). Weaknesses: single-use and short-lived once fired (minutes to an hour); energy density is modest; pyrotechnic activation and thermal management make them strictly specialist devices; costs are defense-grade.

## Examples
Missile and guided-munition power (EaglePicher, ASB/Aerospatiale Batteries, Eurenco), ejection-seat and emergency aircraft systems, nuclear-weapon safing systems, some downhole tools.

## Economic profile
A pure defense market sized by munitions production, currently expanding with global rearmament and deep magazines of precision weapons. Two or three qualified Western suppliers hold the franchise; qualification physics (proving 20-year dormancy) is an unusually literal barrier to entry.
