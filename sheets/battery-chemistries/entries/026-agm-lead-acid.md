## Description
Valve-regulated lead-acid with the electrolyte immobilized in a glass-fiber mat compressed between plates. Oxygen generated on charge recombines internally instead of venting, so the battery is sealed, spill-proof, and maintenance-free, mountable in any orientation. Low internal resistance gives AGM the best high-rate performance in the lead family, which is why start-stop vehicles and UPS strings standardized on it.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Maintenance-free and position-independent; strong high-current capability; better partial-state-of-charge tolerance than flooded (critical for start-stop duty); vibration-resistant plate compression. Weaknesses: more expensive than flooded; intolerant of overcharge (recombination fails, drying the mat); heat accelerates grid corrosion sharply; deep-cycle life remains modest; thermal runaway is possible in float service with failed regulation.

## When to use
Pick AGM where you need lead-acid's cost with high current, no maintenance, and any mounting orientation — start-stop vehicles, UPS strings, motorcycles, marine — and where partial-state-of-charge tolerance matters more than deep cycling. Hold float voltage tightly and keep it cool: overcharge dries the mat and heat corrodes grids fast. Avoid it for deep daily cycling (gel or LFP) and for hot-climate float service; and reassess against lithium 12-V/UPS products yearly, since they are crossing the price line.

## Examples
Start-stop car batteries (the largest AGM market), data-center and telecom UPS strings, motorcycles and marine, premium off-grid installations.

## Economic profile
The premium tier of a commodity industry — roughly 1.5–2× flooded pricing with better margins for the same producers. Start-stop mandates drove a decade of growth; the threat is lithium 12-V and UPS products crossing the price line as cell costs fall.
