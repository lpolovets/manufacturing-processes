## Description
Sodium-ion cells built on polyanionic cathodes — sodium iron pyrophosphate-phosphate (Na4Fe3(PO4)2P2O7, "NFPP"), sodium vanadium phosphates, and fluorophosphates. The sodium analog of LFP's design philosophy: rigid phosphate frameworks trade capacity for outstanding structural stability, thermal safety, and cycle life. NFPP in particular uses only iron and phosphate, targeting the absolute cost floor for grid-duty cells.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Best-in-class cycle life among sodium cathodes (6,000+ cycles demonstrated), excellent thermal stability, air-stable handling, iron-based cost structure. Weaknesses: the lowest energy density of the three sodium cathode families; poor intrinsic conductivity requiring carbon coating and nano-sizing; vanadium versions perform better but import vanadium cost and toxicity, undercutting the cheap-and-clean pitch.

## When to use
Pick NFPP-class polyanion cells for daily-cycled grid storage where $/kWh-cycle is the metric and 6,000+ cycles with iron-phosphate-grade thermal safety pay out over the project life — in practice, mostly where sodium supply chains are mandated or strategically preferred. Avoid it where energy density matters at all (it is the lowest of the sodium families) or where the lowest upfront $/kWh wins the tender; LFP still takes that bid, and layered-oxide sodium serves sodium applications needing more energy.

## Examples
Chinese grid-storage demonstration projects (Datang's 100 MWh-class sodium BESS uses NFPP-family cells); HiNa and Great Power polyanion lines; BYD's sodium roadmap includes polyanion variants.

## Economic profile
The chemistry most explicitly aimed at daily-cycled grid storage, where $/kWh-cycle beats $/kWh and NFPP's cycle life shines. Competes head-to-head with LFP incumbency; wins require sodium's materials savings to survive LFP's relentless cost decline, so most deployments today are strategic or mandate-driven Chinese projects.
