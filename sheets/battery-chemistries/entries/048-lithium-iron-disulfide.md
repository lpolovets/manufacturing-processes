## Description
The premium AA: a lithium anode with an iron disulfide cathode tuned to deliver 1.5 V — deliberately matching alkaline's voltage so lithium performance can drop into legacy devices. Li-FeS2 cells weigh a third less than alkaline, hold several times the capacity under high drain, work at -40 °C, and keep 90% of capacity after 20 years on the shelf.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Best-in-class high-drain and cold performance in the AA/AAA form factor; exceptional shelf life; leakproof construction (no caustic liquid electrolyte). Weaknesses: costs several times alkaline per cell, so it only pays off in demanding devices; 1.8 V open-circuit can confuse voltage-sensing electronics; limited to small formats; non-rechargeable in a world converging on rechargeable everything.

## Examples
Energizer Ultimate Lithium AA/AAA (the defining product), digital cameras and flash units, GPS units and trail cameras, cold-weather expedition equipment, smoke detectors.

## Economic profile
A branded premium segment essentially owned by Energizer, with pricing power that alkaline's commodity market lacks. The niche is stable but capped: high-drain devices increasingly ship with built-in Li-ion, leaving replaceable-battery devices in slow decline.
