## Description
Canada's answer to enrichment scarcity: moderate with heavy water (D2O), which absorbs so few neutrons that natural, unenriched uranium sustains the chain reaction. Fuel sits in hundreds of horizontal pressure tubes rather than one massive vessel, enabling on-power refueling — CANDUs never stop to swap fuel. India industrialized the same lineage into its indigenous PHWR fleet after importing the design in the 1960s.

## Strengths and weaknesses
No enrichment dependency (energy sovereignty was the point), on-power refueling for high availability, pressure-tube construction that avoids giant forgings, and unmatched neutron economy that can burn thorium blends or recycled uranium. Weaknesses: heavy water costs hundreds of millions per plant; pressure tubes embrittle and require mid-life retubing (~$10B-class refurbishments); tritium production demands management; and the excellent neutron economy is proliferation-relevant — India's 1974 test traced to a CANDU-lineage research reactor.

## When to use
Choose a PHWR where enrichment independence is a strategic requirement — the design runs on natural uranium from any supplier — and wherever the CANDU industrial base already exists (Canada, India, Romania, Argentina), where refurbishments and India's 700 MWe builds are proven, competitively priced work. It is also the practical route to burning recycled uranium or thorium blends without new reactor physics. Absent a sovereignty argument or a heavy-water supply chain, the PWR's larger vendor ecosystem makes it the default, and any PHWR business case must carry mid-life retubing as a second capital project.

## Examples
Ontario's Bruce, Darlington, and Pickering fleets (mid-refurbishment through the 2030s); India's 22-unit PHWR fleet and its 700 MWe standardized builds; Qinshan (China), Cernavodă (Romania, units 3–4 revival), Embalse (Argentina).

## Economic profile
India builds 700 MWe PHWRs at roughly $2–3k/kW — among the cheapest credible nuclear anywhere — while Canadian refurbishments (on time, on budget lately) demonstrate rare Western execution. New CANDU sales stalled for decades, but AtkinsRéalis's MONARK 1,000 MWe pitch and Ontario's momentum give the lineage its first real revival window since the 1990s.
