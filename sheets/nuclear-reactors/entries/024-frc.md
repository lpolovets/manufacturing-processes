## Description
A self-organized smoke-ring of plasma: the FRC confines itself with its own internal currents inside a simple cylindrical chamber — no toroidal field coils threading the plasma at all. The geometry's extreme plasma pressure relative to magnetic field (high beta) makes it the natural home for advanced fuels: Helion accelerates two FRCs to collision at a million mph, compresses the merged plasma, and — uniquely in fusion — extracts electricity *directly* as the expanding plasma pushes back on the magnetic field, skipping steam entirely. TAE aims further still, at aneutronic proton-boron fuel.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Simple linear geometry, direct electricity recovery (Helion) that tolerates lower gain, and fuel cycles (D-He3, p-B11) with few or no neutrons — sidestepping tritium logistics and wall damage. Weaknesses: FRC stability and confinement at reactor scale remain the great unknown — the configuration historically decays in milliseconds; advanced fuels demand plasma temperatures 10–50× harsher than D-T; Helion's D-He3 requires breeding helium-3 from D-D reactions in-machine; and the field's boldest commercial promises sit furthest ahead of published physics.

## When to use
Back the FRC camp for the highest-ceiling outcome in fusion: direct electricity conversion and low-neutron fuels would obsolete the steam-cycle plant everyone else is designing — if the self-organized plasma holds together at reactor scale, which remains the great unproven. Underwrite on published triple products and dated, penalty-backed commitments like Helion's Microsoft PPA, not on announcements; the gap between the two is precisely the risk being priced. This is venture-shaped exposure where commercial promises lead the physics — those wanting validation-first fusion belong in tokamaks, and those wanting cheap-hardware optionality in the pulsed camp.

## Examples
Helion (Everett, WA): Polaris machine targeting first fusion electricity, a signed 2028 Microsoft power agreement (fusion's first PPA) and Sam Altman as chairman; TAE Technologies (California, ~$1.2B raised, p-B11 roadmap via its Copernicus machine); University of Washington and PPPL FRC research lineage.

## Economic profile
The venture-capital shape of fusion: highest ceiling (direct conversion, clean fuels, small machines), widest error bars, and commercial commitments that lead the physics rather than trail it. The Microsoft PPA converts credibility risk into a dated, penalty-backed test. Treat published triple-product data — not announcements — as the tracking metric; the gap between the two is the investment.
