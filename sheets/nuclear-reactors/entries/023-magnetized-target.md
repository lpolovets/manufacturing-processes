## Description
The middle path between magnetic and inertial fusion: form a magnetized plasma (typically a compact toroid), then mechanically crush it — with imploding liquid-metal liners, pistons, or pulsed fields — to fusion conditions. The magnetization slows heat loss during compression, so the implosion can be a thousand times slower and cheaper than laser ICF: steam-driven pistons instead of megajoule lasers. General Fusion's design compresses plasma inside a rotating cavity of liquid lithium-lead, which doubles as neutron blanket, tritium breeder, and heat-capture medium in one stroke.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Elegant plant integration — the liquid-metal liner solves first-wall damage, tritium breeding, and heat extraction simultaneously; compression hardware is industrial machinery, not exotic optics. Weaknesses: the target plasma must survive compression without instabilities eating it — the core physics risk, still unproven at scale; repetition-rate engineering (a new plasma every second, in liquid metal) is barely explored; and the field is effectively a handful of companies, so single-company stumbles read as category verdicts.

## When to use
Back MTF when your read is that fusion's graveyard is engineering, not physics: here the plant-killer problems — first-wall damage, tritium breeding, heat capture — are solved in one stroke by the liquid-metal liner, leaving a single concentrated physics question of whether the target plasma survives compression. That inversion suits investors who prefer one binary technical risk over a serial chain of engineering miracles. Size the position for a category that is one or two balance sheets deep; those wanting diversified, physics-validated fusion exposure belong in tokamaks.

## Examples
General Fusion (Vancouver, 20+ years, Bezos-backed; its LM26 machine demonstrated plasma compression milestones in 2025 amid deep funding difficulties — the field's bellwether and its warning), Helion arguably borrows MTF physics in pulsed form, historic LANL FRX-L and Soviet MAGO programs.

## Economic profile
On paper the cheapest path to a fusion *plant* rather than a fusion *experiment*, because everything expensive about tokamaks and lasers is replaced by pistons and pumps. In practice the category's fortunes track one company's balance sheet. The diligence posture: price the plasma-compression physics as the whole risk, because the engineering story, uniquely in fusion, is already plausible.
