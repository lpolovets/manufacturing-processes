## Description
A core made of 400,000 billiard-ball fuel pebbles, each containing thousands of TRISO particles — poppy-seed-sized uranium kernels wrapped in ceramic layers that retain fission products to 1,600 °C+. Helium coolant exits at 750 °C+, and the physics is meltdown-proof in the literal sense: power density is so low and temperature margins so high that a total loss of cooling simply… cools, demonstrated on camera in test reactors. Pebbles circulate continuously, refueling on-line.

## Strengths and weaknesses
The strongest inherent-safety story in fission; high-grade heat for industry and hydrogen; TRISO fuel is its own containment. Weaknesses: energy density is dreadful — huge cores and vessels per MW; TRISO fabrication is expensive and only now scaling in the West; helium leaks through everything; pebble flow and dust caused real headaches in Germany's AVR/THTR (shut down after mechanical and political failures); and HALEU dependence gates every Western project.

## When to use
Choose a pebble-bed HTGR when the product is heat, not just electrons: 550–750 °C steam for chemicals, refining, synfuels, or hydrogen, sold to a creditworthy industrial or hyperscaler offtaker who values the walk-away safety case for close-in siting — it is the right pick wherever emergency-planning-zone size decides site availability. It is the wrong tool for merchant bulk power, where the energy-density penalty makes LWRs cheaper per kW. Any Western schedule is hostage to HALEU and TRISO supply, so secure the fuel line before committing offtake dates.

## Examples
China's HTR-PM at Shidaowan — twin 250 MWt pebble beds, grid-connected 2021, full power 2022: the world's first operating Gen-IV plant, with follow-on HTR-PM600 planned. X-energy's Xe-100 (80 MWe, quad-packs) backed by Amazon and Dow for the Seadrift chemical plant; Germany's AVR/THTR as the instructive ancestors.

## Economic profile
HTR-PM's FOAK cost ran well above Chinese PWRs — the safety case does not yet come cheap. The Western thesis (X-energy) monetizes what PWRs can't: 565–750 °C steam for chemicals, refining, and data-center campuses, sold via corporate offtakes rather than merchant power. TRISO fuel lines (X-energy's TF3, BWXT) are as much the moat as the reactor.
