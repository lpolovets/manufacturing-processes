## Description
Molten salt without the moderator: chloride (rather than fluoride) salts dissolve enough heavy metal to sustain a fast spectrum, uniting liquid fuel's safety and chemistry benefits with a fast reactor's ability to breed and to burn actinide waste. No graphite means no core-life limit, and chloride salts run even hotter than fluorides. The concept is the theoretical endpoint of the fuel cycle — a reactor that could eat spent LWR fuel indefinitely — and almost none of it has ever been built.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Combines drain-tank passive safety with waste transmutation; no fuel fabrication; no graphite replacement; enormous negative feedback from salt expansion. Weaknesses: chloride salts require isotopically enriched chlorine-37 (a brand-new supply chain) to avoid neutron poisoning; corrosion data is thinner than for fluorides; fast-spectrum liquid cores raise novel criticality-safety questions; and startup inventories of fissile material are large — HALEU or plutonium in tonnes, not kilograms.

## When to use
This is the longest-dated bet in the salt family: back it only with decade-plus patience and conviction that a credible waste-burning reactor changes nuclear politics enough to be worth owning early. Gate any commitment on MCRE's results and on three specifics — chlorine-37 enrichment supply, tonnes-scale fissile startup inventory, and chloride corrosion data — rather than the reactor physics, which is not the risk. Buyers wanting molten-salt heat sooner should look to the FHR or thermal MSRs; states wanting actinide burning sooner should look to sodium fast reactors.

## Examples
TerraPower's Molten Chloride Fast Reactor program with its MCRE experiment planned at Idaho National Lab (with Southern Company) — the first fast-spectrum liquid-fuel criticality since the 1960s; Moltex's Stable Salt Reactor (UK/Canada — salt fuel in static pins, a deliberate simplification); Elysium and TerraPower's earlier chloride studies.

## Economic profile
Furthest from revenue of the salt family, but carrying the biggest strategic prize: a credible waste-burning reactor changes the politics of nuclear everywhere. MCRE's results this decade will determine whether the concept graduates from viewgraph to engineering. Diligence weight belongs on chlorine-37 supply, fissile startup inventory, and salt chemistry control — not the reactor physics, which is sound.
