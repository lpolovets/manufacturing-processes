## Description
The anthropomorphic summit: hands with 12–24 actuated degrees of freedom, tendon- or linkage-driven fingers, and (increasingly) dense tactile sensing, built to manipulate anything a human hand can — reorienting objects in-hand, using tools, exploiting every grasp taxonomy entry. For decades a research instrument (Shadow, Allegro, the LEAP wave of cheap open hands), the class exists to answer the field's deepest question: whether general manipulation is a hardware problem, a learning problem, or both.

## Strengths and weaknesses
The only effector class that can attempt the full human task distribution; the substrate for dexterity research (OpenAI's cube, in-hand reorientation, tool use); tactile-rich versions close the loop learning needs. Weaknesses: fragility is structural — dozens of small tendons, joints, and sensors at the collision interface; maintenance is a lab ritual (tendon retensioning, finger swaps); costs run $10k (LEAP-class) to $100k+ (Shadow-class); teleoperation and policies still use a fraction of the DoF; and no industrial task has ever justified one over simpler tooling.

## Examples
Shadow Dexterous Hand (the venerable flagship; OpenAI's Rubik's-cube work), Wonik Allegro (the research default for a decade), LEAP Hand ($2k open-source, democratizing the field), DLR/HIT hands, Sanctuary's hydraulic-tendon Phoenix hands.

## Economic profile
A tiny instrument market (~$10–50M/yr) whose importance is wildly disproportionate: it is the R&D bench for the humanoid hand industry (next entry) and the data source for manipulation foundation models. The LEAP-style cost collapse matters more than any spec sheet — dexterity research at $2k/hand scales in a way $100k/hand never did.
