## Description

Filler metal melts above roughly 450°C and flows into a close-fitting joint while the base materials remain solid.

## Strengths and weaknesses

Joins dissimilar materials, thin parts, and complex assemblies with limited distortion. Joint strength and high-temperature performance may be lower than welding, and gap control is critical.

## When to use

Braze when an assembly has many joints to make in one heat, thin sections, or dissimilar pairs that fusion welding would wreck — plate-fin and tube heat exchangers, carbide inserts on steel bodies, honeycomb panels — using furnace brazing at volume and torch or induction for job work. Design joints for capillary gaps of a few hundredths to a tenth of a millimeter, since gap control is what makes or breaks the joint. Avoid it where service temperature approaches the filler's melting range or full parent-metal strength is required; weld there, and solder when temperatures and loads are lower still.

## Examples

Heat exchangers, carbide tools, HVAC tubing, honeycomb structures.
