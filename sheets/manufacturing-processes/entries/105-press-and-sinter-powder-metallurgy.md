## Description

Metal powder is compacted in a die and heated below its melting point so particles bond.

## Strengths and weaknesses

Offers high material utilization, controlled porosity, and low unit cost at volume. Density and properties may be lower than wrought material, and shapes must permit ejection.

## When to use

Pick press-and-sinter for small-to-medium parts with essentially 2.5D geometry — gears, sprockets, bushings, structural brackets — at tens of thousands of pieces per year and up, where near-net shaping with over 95 percent material utilization undercuts machining from bar, and where controlled porosity is a feature (self-lubricating bearings, filters). The shape must press and eject along one axis: uniform sections, no undercuts or cross-holes without secondary ops. Avoid it below die-amortizing volumes and for fatigue- or impact-critical parts needing full wrought properties, where forging plus machining is the standard; for intricate small 3D geometry at volume, step up to metal injection molding.

## Examples

Gears, bushings, filters, magnets, automotive components.
